How it works

Three steps, one link.

No downloads, no logins, no calendar access. Everything happens in the browser, on a laptop or a phone.

Create the event

Name it, choose the possible days on the calendar and the hours that make sense. Add a short description and your contact details if you like.

Share the link

Send it on WhatsApp, Slack or by email. Each person types their name and paints the slots when they're free, by dragging on desktop or tapping on mobile.

Pick the best time

The group heatmap fills in as answers arrive. Whenly ranks the best slots for you; confirm one and everyone can add it to their calendar.

Features

Small tool, carefully thought through.

Built for freelancers, teams and friends who just need to agree on a time, without the clutter of a full booking platform.

Time zones, handled

Everyone sees the grid in their own local time. Whenly converts automatically, so London, Milan and New York can finally agree.

Live group heatmap

Answers appear in real time. Hover over a slot to see exactly who's in and who's missing, and leave people out of the count with one click.

Best times, ranked

No squinting at colours: the top slots are listed for you and can be filtered by how long the meeting needs to be.

Confirm and add to calendar

The organiser gets a private admin link to lock in the final time. Guests add it to Google Calendar or Outlook, or download an .ics file.

Optional password

Participants can protect their answers with a password to edit them later from any device. No password? No problem: it stays one tap.

Made for phones too

Tap to toggle a slot, press and hold to select a block, swipe to scroll. Sticky headers keep the dates in view on small screens.

Five languages

English, Italian, Spanish, French and German, picked automatically from the device and switchable at any time.

Light and dark

Follows your system theme, or choose one yourself. High-contrast colours and full keyboard and screen-reader support.

Private by design

No tracking, no ads, no accounts. Passwords are hashed, and each event deletes itself the day after its last date.
